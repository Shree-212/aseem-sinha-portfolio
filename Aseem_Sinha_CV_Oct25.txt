BIO

RESEARCH
Summary
I study the interplay of technology and society using a multidisciplinary approach that draws on Computer Science, Economic Sociology, and History. My research examines how information and communication technologies (ICTs) plug into the institutional landscape, changing how economic agents access and recruit key resources. 
Institutions and technologies are mutually constitutive—the soils and seasons that bring forth, sustain, and, in time, undo different organizational forms. They flow into and through each other in continuous feedback loops. The same technology produces different forms across distinct institutional configurations; equally, local institutions accommodate distinct technologies in different ways. Consequently, the resulting ICT-enabled disruption, while seeming ubiquitous, is not uniform. It varies in observable patterns, creating and destroying business models rooted in regions, countries, and ecosystems.
My published work uses configurational theory and methods to identify organizing patterns in factor markets—how smartphones and stock-exchanges produced clear varieties in labour and capital markets respectively. In ongoing work, I extend this configurational lens to AI and ML adoption.
